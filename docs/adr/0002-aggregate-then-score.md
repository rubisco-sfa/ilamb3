# Aggregate errors before scoring within an analysis

A regional score summarizes the errors in many cells and times,
and the score function is nonlinear.
Within each analysis,
ilamb3 reduces the error in each cell to additive statistics,
aggregates them over cells, times, and regions,
and applies the score function once to the aggregate.
Models within an analysis are then ranked by one aggregated error,
and the ranking does not depend on the score function.

## Context

Before [rubisco-sfa/ilamb3#217](https://github.com/rubisco-sfa/ilamb3/pull/217),
ilamb3 scored the error in each cell,
then took the area-weighted mean of the score map as the regional score.
ILAMB v2 did the same (Collier et al., 2018, Eq. 15):
its scalar score is the spatial integral of the score map.
Because the score function $s$ is nonlinear,
the mean of the scores is not the score of the mean error,
and the ranking by mean score can change with the choice of $s$.
The Scoring page sets out the two orders in [Order of operations](../deep-dive/scoring.md#order-of-operations),
and the design doc lists how scores are combined at each level in [How scores are combined now](../design/probabilistic-framework.md#how-scores-are-combined-now).

## Decision

Since rubisco-sfa/ilamb3#217, Bias, RMSE, Hydro, nbp Trajectory,
and Runoff Sensitivity aggregate, then score.
The functions in `ilamb3/analysis/scoring.py` are:

- `scoring.statistics(error, norm, grid)` returns the squared error `mse` and the squared normalizer `mse_ref` in each cell,
  masked to the cells where both are defined.
  Seasonal Cycle and Dispersion, whose scores are affine in their error,
  use `power=1`, which gives `mae` and `mae_ref`.
- `scoring.weighted(stats, weight)` multiplies every statistic by a cell weight,
  which is how mass weighting enters.
- `scoring.aggregate(stats, region)` takes the area-weighted mean of each statistic over a region,
  or with `dim` the mean over dimensions,
  with time steps weighted by their length.
- `scoring.score(stats, method)` applies the score function once,
  to $E = \sqrt{\mathrm{mse} / \mathrm{mse_{ref}}}$,
  or to $\mathrm{mae} / \mathrm{mae_{ref}}$ for `power=1`.
  A result that still varies over cells is flagged as a local score.

For cell $i$ with weight $w_i$, error $e_i$, and normalizer $n_i$,
a regional score is

$$E^2 = \mathrm{NMSE} = \frac{\sum_i w_i e_i^2}{\sum_i w_i n_i^2},
\qquad \text{score} = s(E).$$

`Collier2018` uses $s(E) = e^{-E}$,
and `RegionalQuantiles` and `Linear` use $\max(0, 1 - E)$.

## Justification

Take two models, A and B, and two cells of equal area with $n_i = 1$.
A has errors $(0, 3)$ and B has errors $(1, 1)$.
With $s(E) = e^{-\alpha E}$, the mean of the local scores is

$$\bar s_A = \tfrac{1}{2}\left(1 + e^{-3\alpha}\right),
\qquad \bar s_B = e^{-\alpha}.$$

At $\alpha = 0.25$, $\bar s_A = 0.736$ and $\bar s_B = 0.779$, so B ranks first.
At $\alpha = 4$, $\bar s_A = 0.500$ and $\bar s_B = 0.018$, so A ranks first.
The errors did not change; only $\alpha$ did.
The mean score is the score of the quasi-arithmetic mean of the errors generated by $s$,
so the parameter of the score function also sets how the errors are averaged ([Order of operations](../deep-dive/scoring.md#order-of-operations)).

Aggregating first gives $E_A^2 = 4.5$ and $E_B^2 = 1$.
For every strictly decreasing $s$, $s(E_A) < s(E_B)$,
so B ranks first under every score function and every $\alpha$.
A score function that is only nonincreasing, such as one clipped at 0,
can tie models but cannot reverse their order.

The statistics must be additive for this to hold at every level.
The weighted mean of `mse` over a set of cells is the mean squared error of those cells pooled,
so a regional statistic aggregated from subregions with their total weights,
or a statistic aggregated over time and then over cells,
equals the statistic computed over all the data at once.
This is consistency in aggregation (Vartia, 1976; Diewert, 1978):
a total computed in stages equals the total computed at once.
The squared error has this property because it adds over the data,
as a log-likelihood does ([The design rule](../design/probabilistic-framework.md#the-design-rule)).
Pooling the normalizer weights each cell's squared relative error $(e_i / n_i)^2$ by $w_i n_i^2$,
so a cell where the reference has near-zero variability, such as a desert,
cannot dominate the regional error.

The design doc checks the same rule across the four terms of the MSE ([fix 4](../design/probabilistic-framework.md#fixes-for-each-dataset), `docs/design/experiments/verify_fixes.py`).
For 20 synthetic models,
scoring the total ranks the models identically under $e^{-0.25E}$, $e^{-E}$,
$e^{-4E}$, and $1/(1+E)$.
Averaging the four component scores $e^{-\alpha\sqrt{\text{term}}}$ instead reorders 51 of the 190 pairs of models between $\alpha = 0.25$ and $\alpha = 4$.

## Considered options

- **Average the local scores**,
  as main did before rubisco-sfa/ilamb3#217 and as ILAMB v2 did.
  Its ranking depends on $s$ and on $\alpha$, as the example shows.
- **Average the relative error $E = \sqrt{\mathrm{NMSE}}$ over cells and score the mean**,
  which ranks models independently of $s$, but $E$ is not additive.
  The mean of the cells' $E$ is not the $E$ of the pooled cells:
  a root mean square over time in each cell, averaged over cells,
  differs from the root mean square over all times and cells.
  The squares of the components of the error add, and their square roots do not.
- **Take a median of the scores**, which, taken as an order statistic,
  commutes with a strictly decreasing $s$,
  so its ranking does not depend on $s$.
  The median of the medians of subregions is not the median of the pooled cells,
  so a median is not consistent in aggregation,
  and it does not change when the errors on either side of it change.

## Consequences

- Within an analysis,
  the ranking of models does not depend on the score function or its parameters.
- Regional Bias, RMSE, Hydro, nbp Trajectory,
  and Runoff Sensitivity scores differ from those on main and in ILAMB v2,
  and should not be compared across versions.
  Seasonal Cycle and Dispersion scores are unchanged,
  because they are affine in their errors.
- Score maps remain for display as local scores,
  saved with the statistics they were computed from and refused by every ilamb3 reduction ([ADR 0003](0003-save-statistics-with-score-maps.md)).
- [ADR 0004](0004-aggregate-then-score-across-analyses-and-datasets.md) extends the rule to the Overall Score and to the roll-up across datasets,
  variables, and sections.
